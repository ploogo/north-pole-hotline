On Thanksgiving Day my family got stuck in an airport. Weather delays, a board full of red, and a preschooler who had run out of patience about an hour before we did.

I needed something that would keep her happy and point her energy at something fun. So right there at the gate I built a phone line to Santa.

It's called North Pole Hotline. She taps a big red rotary dial, and Santa picks up and talks with her by voice. It runs on an ElevenLabs voice agent, so it's a real back-and-forth conversation and not a recording.

It's grown a lot in the last 24 hours:

🎅 One tap to call. No app to install, it's just a web page. Allow the mic once and Santa answers.

📝 A secret report for Santa. Behind a "Grown-ups only" button (with a quick math question to keep little fingers out), parents can fill in the child's first name, whether they're on the nice list, what they've been doing well, what to work on, and gift notes. Santa works it into the call and always says his elves told him, never mom or dad.

🎁 Gift steering. If the fire truck is already wrapped, you can tell Santa not to promise a bike.

📲 Share between phones. Both parents can send the report to each other with a link. The secret gift notes are never included in it.

🔒 Privacy kept simple. The report lives on your own phone. There's no account and nothing is saved on a server.

🌙 Day and night. After 5pm it switches to a starry night sky with a moon, and every so often Santa's sleigh flies across the background.

☎️ A daily call limit, so it stays a treat and not a 4-hour marathon.

The hand-drawn look (Santa on the phone, a snowman, a reindeer, a little walking telephone) was made with Higgsfield. The whole thing is one HTML file hosted on GitHub Pages.
